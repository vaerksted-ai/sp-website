Three ways to talk

However you feel like talking, we're here.

Sometimes you want to type quietly next to a sleeping child. Sometimes you just want to hear a calm voice. And sometimes it's easier to show than to explain.

Chat

Type at your own pace and send photos along the way. Great for quiet nights, quick questions and anything you'd like to have in writing afterwards.

Voice call

Just talk, hands free, while you rock a crying baby or pace the hallway. It listens to the cough, asks gentle questions and talks you through what to do.

Video call

Show a rash, a swollen eye or how your child is breathing. The AI can point out what it notices on your camera and guide you through simple checks, like the glass test.

Photo feedback

Snap a photo. See what it sees.

Send a picture of a rash, a bite or a patch of dry skin, and the AI marks up what it notices right on top of your photo: the shape, the size, the spots worth watching. Then it explains in plain words what it might be and what to do. Try an example below.

        For families with small children

        How it helps: on a voice call it can listen to the cough and your child's breathing, and help you keep calm and keep your child calm.

        Get help now: noisy breathing at rest, pulling in between the ribs, drooling or trouble swallowing, pale or blue lips.

        How it helps: advice on pain relief and comfort, and whether it's likely to settle by itself, as most ear infections do within a few days.

        Being honest about the limits

        What an AI practitioner is not allowed to do in Denmark

        Healthcare in Denmark is carefully regulated, and we think that's a good thing. Here's exactly where the line is. For each of these, a licensed doctor takes over.

        Prescribe medicine

        Only authorised doctors (and, within their fields, a few other professions such as dentists) may issue prescriptions (recepter). The AI can explain medicines but can't prescribe, renew or change them.

        Call itself a doctor

        "Læge" is a protected title under the Authorisation Act. On chat, voice and video, the AI always says it's an AI and never poses as a human physician.

        Issue sick notes or certificates

        Medical certificates (lægeerklæringer) for employers, daycare, insurers, municipalities or driving licences must be signed by a doctor who takes professional responsibility.

        Make referrals

        Referrals (henvisninger) to specialists, hospitals, physiotherapists or psychologists must come from a doctor. The AI can tell you when one is likely needed.

        Give a final diagnosis or treat

        Photo and video feedback is an assessment, not a binding diagnosis. Treating illness is reserved for authorised health professionals, and unauthorised treatment that puts people at risk counts as quackery (kvaksalveri).

        Replace your family's own GP

        Your assigned GP (egen læge) in the public health system stays your and your children's GP. We work alongside the public system, not instead of it.

        Handle emergencies

        We're not an emergency service. If the AI notices signs of something serious, such as trouble breathing, a rash that doesn't fade or suicidal thoughts, it stops and tells you to call 112 or a crisis line.

        Examine your child physically

        A camera can't replace hands. Listening to lungs, looking inside ears, blood tests, swabs and vaccinations all happen in a clinic.

        The rules we build under

        Authorisation Act (Autorisationsloven)

        Defines who may practise as a doctor, protects the title "læge", reserves certain activities for doctors and prohibits unauthorised treatment that puts people at risk. It's the main reason the AI assesses and advises, while a licensed doctor makes clinical decisions.

        Health Act (Sundhedsloven)

        Sets out patients' rights: informed consent, confidentiality and access to your own records. Young people aged 15 and over can consent to their own treatment; for younger children, a parent with custody consents. Consultations with our doctors are recorded in a patient record under the Danish record-keeping rules.

        Medicines Act & prescription rules (Lægemiddelloven)

        Only authorised prescribers may prescribe medicines, and prescriptions go through the national electronic prescription system. The AI has no access to issue them.

        EU Medical Device Regulation (MDR 2017/745)

        Software that assesses symptoms or analyses photos of skin is generally a medical device and must be CE-marked before it can be put on the market in the EU. The Danish Medicines Agency (Lægemiddelstyrelsen) supervises medical devices in Denmark. We won't launch before the required conformity assessment is complete.

        EU AI Act

        AI that is, or is part of, a regulated medical device is high-risk, which brings requirements for risk management, data quality, human oversight and logging. The Act also requires people to be told when they're interacting with an AI, which matters especially for our voice and video calls. Our AI's face and voice are always labelled as AI.

        GDPR & the Danish Data Protection Act

        Health data, including photos of your skin and your children's, is a special category of personal data (GDPR Art. 9). It's processed only on a valid legal basis, kept in the EU, encrypted, never sold, and never used for advertising or training without explicit consent. You can see, export and delete it.

        Supervision by the Danish Patient Safety Authority

        The doctors behind the service are supervised by Styrelsen for Patientsikkerhed, and online health services must be registered as healthcare providers (behandlingssteder). Adverse events are reported and handled as in any other Danish clinic.

        Marketing of health services

        Danish rules require marketing of health services to be accurate and not misleading. That's why this page is clear about what the AI can't do, and why we don't promise outcomes.

        A summary of the regulatory framework for information only, not legal advice. The final service design is subject to review by legal counsel and the relevant Danish authorities.

        FAQ

        Questions you might have

        Is it a real doctor?

        No, and it'll always tell you so. Synthetic Practitioner is an AI trained to hold a medical conversation the way a kind, thorough GP would. The face and voice on video and voice calls are generated by AI too. When a licensed doctor is needed, you're handed over to a real person, and it's always clear who you're talking to.

        Can I use it for my children?

        Yes, that's one of the main reasons we're building it. Parents will be able to add their children to their account through MitID. For babies and very young children, the AI is extra cautious and quicker to recommend a human doctor.

        What happens to the photos I send?

        They're used for your consultation only, stored encrypted in the EU as part of your record, and you can delete them. They're never shared, sold or used to train the AI without your explicit consent.

        Do I need video for it to work?

        Not at all. Chat works everywhere, voice is great when your hands are full, and video is there when showing is easier than explaining.

        When will it launch?

        We're still building it. We'll open to waitlist members in small groups once the service has passed the required medical device and safety approvals, and we'll email you before that happens.

        What will it cost?

        Pricing isn't set yet. Our aim is for talking to the AI practitioner to cost much less than a private online consultation, with a clear price for any handover to a human doctor.
