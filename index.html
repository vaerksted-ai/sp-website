What it does

The everyday questions you'd bring to your GP

Most visits to a general practitioner are about common, manageable problems. Synthetic Practitioner is built to handle that first conversation well, and to know when it isn't enough.

🩺

Symptom assessment & triage

Describe what's wrong in your own words. It asks structured follow-up questions and tells you how urgent it is: self-care, see a doctor this week, call the out-of-hours service now, or call 112.

🤧

Common, minor illnesses

Colds, flu, sore throat, cough, earache, stomach bugs, urinary discomfort, rashes, allergies. Clear advice on self-care, over-the-counter options and warning signs to watch for.

📋

Understanding test results

Got lab results on sundhed.dk or a letter from the hospital you don't understand? It explains the terms and numbers in plain language and suggests what to ask your doctor.

💊

Medication questions

How and when to take a medicine, common side effects, interactions with other drugs or alcohol, and what to do if you missed a dose. General information, not a new prescription.

📈

Living with a chronic condition

Support between check-ups for conditions like type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, asthma and COPD: logging readings, spotting trends and knowing when to contact your own doctor.

🧠

Mental wellbeing

A calm place to talk about stress, sleep problems, low mood or anxiety, with evidence-based self-help, and a direct route to professional help and crisis lines when needed.

👶

Children & family

Fever in a toddler at 02:00, rashes, vaccination programme questions, normal development. Helps parents decide whether it can wait until morning.

✈️

Prevention & travel

Healthy lifestyle advice, screening programmes you're eligible for, and what to think about before travelling, including which vaccinations to discuss with a clinic.

📝

Prepare for your real appointment

Get a clear, structured summary of your symptoms and history to share with your own GP or a hejdoktor.dk doctor, so the consultation starts where it matters.

Legal limits in Denmark

What an AI doctor is not allowed to do

Healthcare in Denmark is tightly regulated, and for good reason. We think you should know exactly where the line is. These are the things Synthetic Practitioner will not do on its own. When you need one of them, a licensed doctor takes over.

✕ Prescribe medicine

Only authorised doctors (and, within their fields, dentists and a few other professions) may issue prescriptions (recepter). The AI can explain medicines but cannot prescribe, renew or change them.

✕ Call itself a doctor

"Læge" is a protected title under the Authorisation Act. Only people authorised by the Danish Patient Safety Authority may use it. Our AI is always clearly identified as an AI, never as a human physician.

✕ Issue sick notes or medical certificates

Medical certificates (lægeerklæringer) for employers, insurers, municipalities or driving licences must be issued and signed by a doctor who takes professional responsibility.

✕ Make referrals

Referrals (henvisninger) to specialists, hospitals, physiotherapy or psychologists with public subsidy must come from a doctor. The AI can tell you when a referral is likely needed.

✕ Give a final diagnosis or treatment

It gives an assessment of what is likely and how urgent it is, not a binding diagnosis. Treating illness is reserved for authorised health professionals, and acting as if unauthorised can be treated as quackery (kvaksalveri).

✕ Replace your own GP

Your assigned GP (egen læge) under the public health insurance scheme stays your GP. Synthetic Practitioner sits alongside the public system and does not replace it.

✕ Handle emergencies

It is not an emergency service. If it detects signs of something serious, such as chest pain, stroke symptoms or suicidal thoughts, it stops and tells you to call 112 or the relevant crisis line.

✕ Examine you physically

No blood tests, swabs, vaccinations, listening to your lungs or feeling your stomach. Anything that requires hands-on examination goes to a clinic.

The rules we build under

Authorisation Act (Autorisationsloven)

Defines who may practise as a doctor, protects the title "læge", reserves certain activities for doctors and prohibits unauthorised treatment that puts people at risk. It is the main reason the AI assesses and advises, while a licensed doctor makes clinical decisions.

Health Act (Sundhedsloven)

Sets out patients' rights: informed consent, confidentiality and access to your own health records. Consultations handled by our doctors are recorded in a patient record under the Danish record-keeping rules (journalføringspligt).

Medicines Act & prescription rules (Lægemiddelloven)

Only authorised prescribers may prescribe medicines, and prescriptions are sent electronically through the national prescription system. The AI has no access to issue prescriptions.

EU Medical Device Regulation (MDR 2017/745)

Software that helps assess or triage symptoms is generally a medical device and must be CE-marked before it can be put on the market in the EU. The Danish Medicines Agency (Lægemiddelstyrelsen) supervises medical devices in Denmark. Synthetic Practitioner will not launch before the required conformity assessment is complete.

EU AI Act

AI systems that are, or are part of, regulated medical devices are classified as high-risk. That brings requirements for risk management, data quality, human oversight, transparency and logging. You will always be told that you're talking to an AI.

GDPR & the Danish Data Protection Act

Health data is a special category of personal data (GDPR Art. 9). We only process it with a valid legal basis, keep it within the EU, encrypt it, and never sell it or use it for advertising. You can see, export and delete your data.

Supervision by the Danish Patient Safety Authority

The doctors behind the service are supervised by Styrelsen for Patientsikkerhed, and online health services must be registered as healthcare providers (behandlingssteder). Adverse events are reported and handled like in any other Danish clinic.

Marketing of health services

Danish rules require marketing of health services to be accurate and not misleading. That is why this page says clearly what the AI cannot do, and why we make no promises about outcomes.

This is a summary of the regulatory framework for information purposes and is not legal advice. The final service design is subject to review by legal counsel and the relevant Danish authorities.

FAQ

Questions we get a lot

Is this a real doctor?

No. Synthetic Practitioner is an AI. It's trained to hold a medical conversation the way a good general practitioner would, but it is not a licensed doctor. When a licensed doctor is needed, you are handed over to a human, and it's always clear which one you're talking to.

When will it launch?

We are pre-product. We'll open to waitlist members in small groups once the service has passed the required medical device and safety approvals. We'll email you before that happens.

What will it cost?

Pricing isn't set yet. Our aim is that talking to the AI practitioner is significantly cheaper than a private online consultation, with a transparent price for any handoff to a human doctor.

Does it work with the public health system?

It works alongside it. Your own GP stays your GP. You can take the written summary to any doctor, and we will work towards letting you share it through official channels.

What happens to my data?

Your health data is encrypted, stored in the EU and processed under GDPR. It is never sold or used for advertising. For the waitlist, we only store the details you enter in the form below.

Who is behind it?

Synthetic Practitioner is developed by vaerksted, building on the clinical and operational experience of hejdoktor.dk.
